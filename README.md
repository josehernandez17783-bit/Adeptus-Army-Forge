## 🌌 Overview

**Warhammer 40K: Tactical Cohort Forge** is a *reimagined successor spirit* to the classic **Warhammer-40K-Unit-Commander-Console** project. Where the original sought to organize armies and parse the rubble of a hundred battles, this new repository aims to turn every skirmish into a curated chronicle — a doctrine engine that learns from your matches, polishes your roster logic, and hands you the keys to the grimoire of your own command style.

Think of it as the difference between a ledger and an oracle. The old console tallied. This one *interprets*.

Whether you are orchestrating a narrative crusade across a dozen sectors of a homemade map, or running a tight tournament bracket with friends on a weekend, Tactical Cohort Forge gives your tabletop warfare a digital spine — recording decisions, projecting outcomes, and offering tactical suggestions drawn from your own historical patterns.

The project is built with a philosophy borrowed from ancient war rooms: *"A commander who does not study their own battles will lose the same one twice."* This console is that study hall, rendered in pixels.

## 🎯 Why This Exists

The original **Warhammer-40K-Unit-Commander-Console** answered a simple question: *"Can I keep my units organized without drowning in spreadsheets?"* The answer, for many commanders, was yes — but it left another question unanswered: *"Can I understand why I won or lost last Tuesday?"*

Tactical Cohort Forge was born from that second question. It treats every match as a data point and every roster as a hypothesis. When you load an army list, you are not just populating a table — you are proposing a theory about how the battlefield will unfold. The Forge tests that theory against your own history and gently nudges you toward sharper decisions.

This is not a replacement for your rulebooks, your dice, or your imagination. It is a companion — a quiet adjutant standing at your shoulder, whispering, *"Commander, you have deployed that pattern before. Perhaps a flanking variant?"*

## 🧠 Doctrine Engine Deep Dive

The Doctrine Engine is the heart of this console — and the feature that most clearly separates it from a plain unit tracker.

Imagine you have fought forty battles. The engine has seen them all. It notices that your armored spearhead formations perform unusually well when the sector includes dense cover, but falter in open corridors. It notices that your lighter skirmish detachments tend to survive longer when kept in reserve for the first two turns. It assembles these observations into **doctrine cards** — small, digestible tactical proposals.

A doctrine card is not an order. It is an invitation to consider. Each card includes:

1. **Observation** — the pattern the engine detected.
2. **Confidence Band** — how strongly your own history supports the pattern.
3. **Suggested Adaptation** — a concrete tweak you might try next time.
4. **Counter-Signal** — a situation where the pattern might betray you.

This four-part structure is deliberate. A tactician who only hears *"do this"* becomes dependent. A tactician who hears *"this worked, unless that happens"* becomes adaptive. The Forge aims for the latter.

The engine runs entirely offline. No cloud tether, no telemetry, no external model. Your battles remain yours.

## 📊 Battle Ledger & Analytics

The Battle Ledger is where discipline meets memory. Every engagement you log is stamped with a date, a sector, an objective set, two or more rosters, and a free-form narrative field for the moments that numbers cannot hold — the improbable charge, the miracle save, the commander who held the line with a single surviving model.

Above the ledger sits the **Analytics Dashboard**, a visual stratum where patterns rise to the surface:

- **Unit Performance Heatmap** — see at a glance which units consistently deliver and which quietly underperform.
- **Casualty Curves** — track how attrition unfolds across turns, per faction, per list.
- **Objective Capture Rate** — a measure of how often you actually secure what you set out to secure.
- **Opponent Profile Matrix** — understand the tendencies of your regular rivals without reducing them to a single number.
- **Trend Lines Across a Season** — watch your own command style evolve over months.

All of it exports to readable formats so you can paste summaries into a club newsletter, a narrative campaign journal, or your own private war diary.

## 🌍 Multilingual Command Bridge

A battlefield has no native tongue. Commanders gather from every corner, and the console should welcome them all. The Multilingual Command Bridge ensures the interface, tooltips, doctrine cards, and export templates all render cleanly in each supported language.

The bridge is built on a translation layer that avoids hard-coded strings entirely. Every label is a key; every key has a home in a translation catalog. Adding a new language is a matter of supplying one catalog file — no source modifications required.

This design means the console can grow alongside its community, absorbing regional phrasings and idiom without fracturing the core experience.

## 📱 Responsive Command Interface

A command post is not always a desk. Sometimes it is a kitchen table at midnight, a tablet balanced on a rulebook, or a laptop squeezed between two stacks of miniatures. The interface bends to the setting rather than demanding the setting bend to it.

- **Adaptive Grid Layout** — panels rearrange themselves based on available space rather than forcing scrollbars.
- **Touch-Ready Controls** — large, forgiving targets for tablet use during live play.
- **Keyboard-First Shortcuts** — for commanders who prefer to keep both hands near the dice tray.
- **Dark and Light Campaign Themes** — because a war room at 2 a.m. should not glare.
- **Font Scaling** — readable whether you are on a 13-inch screen or a wall-mounted display.

The goal is simple: never let the software become the obstacle between you and the next turn.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Is this a replacement for my rulebook?**
No. It assumes you already know how to play. It helps you remember, analyze, and refine how *you* play.

**Where is my data stored?**
Locally, on your own machine, by default. Nothing leaves your device unless you export it yourself.

**Can I use this for editions other than the current one?**
Yes. Rosters, doctrines, and ledgers are not edition-locked. You define the rules context you are playing in.

**Is there a mobile version?**
The responsive interface works well on tablets and phones, though some analytics views are happiest on larger screens.

**Can I contribute a translation or a doctrine pack?**
Absolutely. Community contributions are the lifeblood of this project.

**Is internet access required?**
No. The console is designed to function offline. Connectivity only matters if you choose to share exports.
